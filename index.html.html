EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Space Survival Challenge & Project Objectives

The Problem of Deep Space Logistics: Resupplying a human habitat on Mars or the Moon with pre-packaged meals from Earth is economically and biologically impossible long-term. Transporting 1 kg of mass to deep space costs between $10,000 and $20,000. For a 4-person crew on a 1,000-day Mars mission, food alone would weigh over 10,000 kg and cost upwards of $150 Million just for shipping!

Furthermore, space radiation breaks down essential vitamins (such as Vitamin B1, Vitamin C, and Folate) in stored food packets after 12 to 18 months, leading to nutrient loss and scurvy risks for astronauts.

The Core Idea: Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS): This project proposal, titled "COSMIC SUSTENANCE," designs a 100% self-sustaining circular agricultural ecosystem. By utilizing plant photosynthesis, biological waste treatment, and advanced microgravity water harvesting, we convert human breathing waste ($CO_2$), human metabolic waste (feces & urine), and toxic Martian dust into continuous fresh oxygen, pure drinking water, and nutrient-dense crops!

💨 1. Oxygen Generation Mechanics

Oxygen is produced via two complementary systems:

Water Electrolysis: 2H₂O (Liquid) + Solar Power ➔ 2H₂ (Gas) + O₂ (Breathable)
Plant Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Photons ➔ C₆H₁₂O₆ (Sugar) + 6O₂ (Oxygen)

*Note: Plants absorb astronaut CO2 through stomata leaves, returning clean O2 back to the cabin.

💧 2. Water Recovery & Sabatier Loop

98% of habitat water is continuously purified through:

  • Transpiration Condensation: Plant leaves release clean water vapor that is collected by atmospheric dehumidifiers.
  • Sabatier Chemical Reactor:
    CO₂ + 4H₂ ➔ CH₄ (Methane Vent) + 2H₂O (Water)
  • Urine Vacuum Distillation: Reclaims 98% pure drinking water from human wastewater.

5. METHOD OF GROWING & SOIL GENESIS

Microgravity Capillary Wicking & Turning Human Waste into Arable Soil (Pages 7–8)

The Microgravity Fluid Challenge: On Earth, gravity pulls water down into the soil, while plant roots feel gravity using starch grains (statoliths) to grow downwards. In zero gravity ($0g$), water surface tension causes liquid to form floating spheres that envelope roots, cutting off oxygen and drowning plants!

The Solution: PONDS Passive Capillary Systems & Aeroponics: We use NASA PONDS pads (Passive Orbital Nutrient Delivery System) made of porous ceramic and clay sponges. Capillary action draws moisture directly to roots without electrical pumps, while overhead red/blue LED lights guide stem direction (phototropism).

7. FOOD SCIENCE TECHNOLOGIES

3D Food Printing, Microgravity Cooking, Hyperspectral AI & Biosecurity (Pages 11–13)

Microgravity Culinary Physics: On Earth, floating food particles fall to the table. In microgravity, breadcrumbs float into air ducts, clogging life support pumps and creating fire hazards! Furthermore, liquids do not stay in open frying pans.

🍕

3D Food Printing Paste Extrusion

Dehydrated plant powders, microalgae proteins, and water pastes are extruded layer-by-layer to create familiar textures such as space pizzas, noodles, and customized nutrition bars.

🍳

Centrifugal Artificial Gravity Frying

Using spinning appliances (such as the SATED cooker), centrifugal force pushes liquid batter and oil against heated pan walls, simulating 1g cooking physics in zero gravity.

🤖

AI Hyperspectral Disease Scanning

Infrared cameras monitor leaf chlorophyll fluorescence. Computer vision models detect fungal spore infection or nitrogen deficiency up to 14 days before visible to human eyes.

💡

Night UV-C Sterilization Sweeps

Automated LED arrays sweep crops with UV-C germicidal light during dark sleep cycles. Because fungal spores cannot repair DNA damage without blue light, this sanitizes mold without toxic chemicals.

8. CONCLUSION

Synthesis of Findings & Multi-Planetary Vision (Pages 14–15)

Establishing permanent human bases on the Moon and Mars requires moving away from linear supply chains ("bring everything from Earth") and embracing circular bio-regenerative architecture.

This research report, "COSMIC SUSTENANCE," demonstrates that through the integrated application of water electrolysis, Sabatier carbon dioxide recycling, PONDS microgravity fluidics, bacterial regolith detoxification (*Azospira oryzae*), and supercritical water oxidation (SCWO) of organic waste, a space habitat can sustain human life indefinitely.

Key Project Takeaways for "Live in a Healthy Space" Judges:

  • 100% Closed Oxygen & Water Loop: Photosynthesis replaces mechanical air scrubbers while generating fresh Vitamin C and Folate to prevent astronaut scurvy.
  • Turning Waste into Fertile Soil: Toxic Martian perchlorates are biologically converted into harmless salt ($Cl^-$) and breathable oxygen, while human waste is sterilized into organic crop fertilizer.
  • Microgravity Fluid Mechanics: Passive PONDS capillary wicking pads eliminate root drowning risks in zero gravity without complex electric pumps.
  • Psychological & Morale Boost: Harvesting crisp, brightly colored microgreens and cherry tomatoes boosts astronaut psychological health during long 1,000-day deep space isolation.
